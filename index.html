        You may qualify for health coverage at no cost.

        Virginia Medicaid covers adults 19 to 64 with income under 138% of the federal poverty level. Children and pregnant people can qualify with higher incomes through Medicaid or FAMIS. Applying is free.

        Apply online at CommonHelp Call Cover Virginia: 1-855-242-8282
        • Cover Virginia answers calls Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 7 p.m. (TTY: 1-888-221-1590). You can also apply through your local Department of Social Services.
        • A decision can take up to 45 days. Health Brigade and CrossOver can help their patients apply.
        • Starting January 2027, adults covered through Medicaid Expansion will have work and community engagement requirements. They don’t apply to children, pregnant and postpartum people, or adults with a disability.

        Before your first visit

        Most free clinics need you to register before you can be seen. These steps save a wasted trip.

        1. Call first

          Hours, rules and openings change. Ask if they’re taking new patients and how to register. Many clinics need a registration or screening appointment before your first visit.

        2. Ask who they serve

          Some places are only for people without insurance, or below a set income. Others take Medicaid and private insurance too. The listings here show what each place told the public.

        3. Gather your papers

          Bring what the clinic asks for — usually an ID and proof of income. Don’t let a missing document stop you from calling; ask what they can accept instead.

        4. Plan your trip

          Use the Directions button on any listing. Several clinics are on GRTC bus routes, and Capital Area Health Network offers a transportation service for its patients.

        What to bring, if you have it

        • Photo ID — Health Brigade accepts expired IDs from any government
        • Proof of income, such as recent pay stubs or a benefits letter
        • Your Medicaid, FAMIS or insurance card
        • A list of the medicines you take, or the bottles themselves
        • Children’s immunization records, for kids’ visits

        Need an interpreter? Ask when you call. Bon Secours’ Care-A-Van, for example, offers interpreters in more than 150 languages.

        When it can’t wait

        This directory can’t give medical advice or tell you whether something is an emergency. If you think it is, call 911.

        • Life-threatening emergency 911 Chest pain, trouble breathing, severe bleeding, overdose
        • Mental health or suicidal crisis 988 Call or text, 24/7. Chat at 988lifeline.org
        • Richmond crisis line (RBHA) (804) 819-4100 24/7, with mobile crisis outreach in the city
        • Poison or overdose questions 1-800-222-1222 Poison Help — free and confidential, 24/7
        • Help finding any service 211 211 Virginia — free, confidential, 24/7

        Under federal law, hospital emergency departments must screen and stabilize anyone with an emergency medical condition, whether or not they can pay.
